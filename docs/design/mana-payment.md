# Design note — mana payment enumeration (P2.1)

The reference for the payment model built in P2.1 and extended by P2.2 (real basics) and
Phase 5 (triggered mana abilities, additional/alternative costs). PLAN.md §7 names payment
combinatorics a top risk; the mitigation is this model: **declarative plans over collapsed
source classes**, enumerated exhaustively, chosen by index (ADR-005).

## The payment-plan model

A **payment plan** is a declarative description of how one `ManaCost` will be paid — chosen
*before* anything is executed, surfaced as the enumerated options of a `ChoosePaymentPlan`
request. A plan assigns each cost symbol one **payment**:

- `WithMana(manaType, source)` — one mana of a specific `ManaType`, drawn either
  `FromPool` (mana already in the caster's pool) or `ByTapping` a **source class** (below).
- `WithTwoLife` — the CR 107.4-Phyrexian alternative: pay 2 life instead of the mana.

A generic symbol `{N}` expands to `N` independent single-mana payments ( `{0}` to none), so a
plan is a flat list aligned with the cost's symbols in printed order, generics expanded in
place. A plan is legal iff every payment satisfies its symbol — `{R}` demands `RED`; `{C}`
demands `COLORLESS` specifically (CR 107.4c); `{G/U}` accepts `GREEN` or `BLUE`; `{R/P}`
accepts `RED` or `WithTwoLife`; generic accepts any type — the tapped sources exist untapped
on the battlefield under the caster's control, pool draws exist in the pool, and the summed
life payments do not exceed the caster's life total (CR 118.8; paying *down to* 0 or into a
lethal SBA is legal, per CR 704.5a the death follows).

Execution is separate and mechanical (CR 601.2g–h): for each `ByTapping` payment the engine
taps the class's first untapped member in battlefield order, resolves its mana ability
immediately — no stack, no priority (CR 605.3) — adds the produced mana to the pool, then
pays the cost from the pool and deducts any life payments. The plan is *what*; the pipeline
owns *when and how*.

## Equivalence and collapsing

Without collapsing, "tap 2 of my 5 Mountains for `{1}{R}`" is C(5,2)·permutations of plans
that differ only in which physically-identical land taps. The model collapses them at the
representation level: a plan never names a source object. It names a **source class** — the
equivalence class of interchangeable sources — and execution picks concrete members
deterministically.

**Equivalence relation.** Two untapped battlefield objects controlled by the caster are
payment-equivalent iff they have the same printed card (`CardRef`) **and** the same
*production profile* — the canonical list of mana-type options their tap-for-mana abilities
can add. Same-profile is the load-bearing half (it is what makes tapping either
indistinguishable in every future game state); same-card is a deliberate safety margin: costs
that care about the card itself (Fireblast's "sacrifice two Mountains", Lava Dart's
flashback — docs/decklists.md) can never be wronged by an over-eager merge, at the price of
occasionally enumerating two plans where one would do. Phase 5 refines the profile, never
the relation: an Utopia-Sprawl-enchanted Forest has a *different* profile from a bare Forest
(its tap also yields the Sprawl trigger's mana), so it lands in a different class
automatically once profiles are computed through the continuous-effect layer.

**Stable ordering** (ADR-005 indices; ADR-006 replay). Source classes are ordered by first
appearance in battlefield order (insertion-stable, see the `GameState` iteration rule). Plans
are generated by depth-first search over the cost's symbols in printed order; each symbol's
candidate payments are tried in a fixed order — mana types in WUBRG-then-colorless order,
`FromPool` before `ByTapping`, classes in their battlefield order, `WithTwoLife` last — and
within a run of *identical* symbols assignments are constrained to be non-decreasing in that
candidate order. The result is a duplicate-free, deterministically ordered plan list; equal
states always enumerate equal lists.

## The cost shapes, and why they fit

- **Hybrid `{G/U}`** — the symbol accepts two mana types; a plan records which side was
  chosen because the `WithMana` payment names its type. Both colors available ⇒ exactly two
  plans (they leave genuinely different battlefields).
- **Phyrexian `{R/P}`** — `WithTwoLife` is a peer payment, not a special case; the life-total
  bound is checked at enumeration so an unaffordable life plan never appears.
- **Any-color sources** — a source's profile lists every type it can produce; the plan's
  `WithMana` type fixes the choice, so "add one mana of any color" needs no extra machinery.
- **`{C}`** — `COLORLESS` is an ordinary `ManaType`; `{C}` simply demands it while generic
  accepts it, exactly the CR 107.4c distinction the core mana model already draws.

## Triggered mana abilities mid-payment (Phase 5)

Utopia Sprawl's "whenever enchanted Forest is tapped for mana, add one mana of the chosen
color" (CR 605.1b) slots into **execution**, not the plan shape: resolving a tap fires any
triggered mana abilities immediately — again no stack, no priority (CR 605.3) — before
payment draws from the pool. The extra mana lands in the pool; whatever payment does not
consume floats until the step ends (CR 500.4). Plans stay declarative and single-mana-typed;
the only Phase 5 changes are (a) profiles computed from *all* abilities a tap sets off, which
the equivalence relation already keys on, and (b) the pool-emptiness invariant gaining its
declared float exception. Nothing reshapes.

## Why enumeration completeness is testable

The plan space for a fixed `(cost, sources, pool, life)` is finite and small, so tests keep a
**brute-force oracle**: naively generate *every* raw assignment (each symbol × each payable
option, no cleverness), canonicalize each by the same ordering rules, deduplicate, and
set-compare against the enumerator's output. Equality proves both directions at once — no
missing plan, no phantom plan, collapsing exactly right. On top of that sit the two
end-to-end completeness properties (ADR-005): every enumerated cast option must execute
through the full CR 601 pipeline without error (no phantoms), and constructed scenarios
where a cast is legal/illegal must/must-not enumerate it (no gaps). The fuzz harness then
defends both across seeds.
